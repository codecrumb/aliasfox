Who runs this

This site is run by the AliasFox project (GitHub owner: codecrumb). AliasFox is unofficial and not affiliated with or endorsed by Apple. Its source code is public at github.com/codecrumb/aliasfox, so you can check what this policy says against what the code does.

This policy covers the AliasFox service at {{PUBLIC_URL}}.

What AliasFox stores

For each account, the AliasFox database holds:

  • an identifier from the sign-in provider (an opaque user id, not your email address);
  • a wrapped data key: a random key made for you, itself encrypted with a key that only the server has;
  • one encrypted Apple session, once you connect Apple: the cookies iCloud gives a signed-in browser, plus the iCloud service address, your Apple account id and, when Apple issues one, a trust token. Only the latest one is kept;
  • the API keys you create, if any: a name you choose, the permissions you give it, an optional expiry, and when it was created and last used. The key itself is shown to you once and only a keyed hash of it is stored, so AliasFox cannot show it to you again;
  • timestamps (when your account was created and when your Apple session was last saved) and a counter that is increased when you log out, so logging out ends every open session.

What AliasFox does not store

  • Your Apple password is never stored. It is used once, in server memory, to log in to Apple, and kept only until the login finishes or is abandoned (at most about ten to eleven minutes while waiting for your two-factor code). It is not written to the database, to disk or to logs.
  • Your aliases, labels and notes are not stored on the server. To keep the app fast, the server keeps your alias list in memory after fetching it from Apple and reuses it for up to 60 seconds. It is removed from memory within about two minutes of your last use, and when you log out, disconnect Apple or delete your account (a request already in flight at that moment can briefly put the list back; it is dropped within about two minutes), and lost when the server restarts. It is never written to the database or to disk.
  • Your browser keeps an encrypted copy of your alias list (in IndexedDB) so the app opens quickly, plus any changes you made offline that are waiting to sync. It is cleared when you log out.
  • AliasFox does not write IP addresses, request lines or cookies to its database or its own logs. IP addresses are held in server memory only, for rate limiting (to slow down password guessing and abuse), and are never written to the database or to disk. Each is dropped within about two minutes after its limit window ends, and all are cleared when the server restarts.

Your Apple session and its risks

Please read this before you connect Apple.

  • The server holds the key that decrypts the stored Apple sessions. Your session is encrypted in the database, but if someone obtained both the database and the server secret, they could decrypt every stored session, including yours. A leak of the database alone does not expose them.
  • While the server handles one of your requests, your Apple session is decrypted in server memory.
  • An Apple session grants broader iCloud web access than Hide My Email. AliasFox uses Apple only to sign you in, check the session and manage Hide My Email, but whoever holds the session could use it for other iCloud web services until it expires.
  • Apple sees the requests coming from the server's datacenter address, not from your device. Many users share that address, and Apple may slow down, challenge or block it.
  • Nothing uses your Apple session while you are away: the server talks to Apple only while handling a request made by you in the app, or by an API key you created (see below).

API keys

  • An API key lets a program (a password manager, a script, an AI assistant) act on your aliases without your password. What it can do is limited to the permissions you chose when you created it (read, create, edit, deactivate, delete). Requests made with a key use your stored Apple session, the same way requests from the app do.
  • Whoever holds a key can do what its permissions allow until you revoke it or it expires. The program you give the key to keeps its own copy, which AliasFox cannot protect. Give each key the fewest permissions it needs.
  • A key is deleted when you revoke it, and deleting your account deletes all your keys. If your account is suspended, your keys are deleted when the server starts. If the server's secret key is ever changed, every key stops working.
  • Keys do not change what is stored about your aliases: the alias list is still not stored, only kept in memory for up to 60 seconds as described above. Results sent to AI assistants through a key leave out your forwarding address.

Sign-in provider and hosting

  • Sign-up and log-in are handled by Neon Auth, run by Neon. Neon holds your email address and a hash of your password (the password hash), not AliasFox. When you sign up, the part of your email address before the @ is sent to the sign-in provider as the account's display name, because Neon Auth requires a name.
  • The service is hosted on Render (the web server) and Neon (the database). Both keep their own request and system logs, which can include IP addresses, under their own retention policies. AliasFox adds no IP storage of its own to its database.

Cookies

AliasFox sets one strictly necessary session cookie that keeps you logged in. It is HttpOnly (scripts cannot read it) and SameSite=Strict (it is not sent with requests from other sites). The sign-in provider may set its own sign-in cookies while you log in. There is no tracking: no analytics, no advertising, no third-party scripts or fonts.

Disconnect or delete everything

  • Disconnect Apple: in the app, open Account and choose Disconnect Apple. This deletes your stored Apple session. Your AliasFox account stays.
  • Delete account: in the app, open Account and choose Delete account. This deletes your stored Apple session, your API keys, your wrapped data key and your user record from the AliasFox database, and logs you out.
  • AliasFox asks the sign-in provider (Neon) to delete your sign-in record when you delete your account. If that request does not go through, the app tells you, and it is removed on request. {{CONTACT}}
